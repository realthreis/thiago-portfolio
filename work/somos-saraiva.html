Head of Marketing, Higher Education · Jul 2016 – Oct 2017

Launching a Data-Driven Legal Ed-Tech Product Inside a Century-Old Publishing House

A new category for a 100+ year old legal publisher, launched with persona-first planning and CRO discipline — plus a creative-risk outlier that shipped Brazil's first VR book trailer.

Context

This was a single role — Head of Marketing, Higher Education — covering the publishing and digital-products side of SOMOS's higher-ed portfolio: Editora Saraiva (the flagship legal/higher-ed publisher) and Editora Érica (technical/vocational). Benvirá, a general consumer-audience publishing imprint, sat under the same management umbrella and the same marketing mandate — the deliberate outlier in this case study, included because it shipped some of the most creatively ambitious work of the period, not because it shared an audience with the rest of the portfolio.

Saraiva Aprova — launching Brazil's first ML-powered adaptive bar exam platform

The product Saraiva Aprova, an adaptive bar-exam (OAB) prep platform incorporating machine learning and gamification — a new category for a 100+ year old legal publisher to enter.
How it launched Persona-first planning and CRO discipline from day one — landing page formats were A/B tested rather than shipped on instinct. Result: 1,000 students at launch, and the 12-month enrollment target was hit in under 6 months.
The content engine behind it More sophisticated than a typical launch calendar: data as social proof (a pass-rate-by-attempt chart used directly in feed content), urgency mechanics (exam-countdown content), cross-sell bundling within Saraiva's own legal ecosystem, real video production (not just static graphics), an instructor showcase for trust-building, and a dedicated blog covering OAB exam content — positioning Saraiva Aprova as a genuine content destination, not just a product page. Covered by PublishNews, regarded as the most prestigious publishing-industry newsletter in Brazil.

Saraiva brand strategy — building a "lovemark" for lawyers

Alongside the product launch, I worked on Saraiva's broader brand positioning for its legal-professional audience — a deliberate strategy to keep Saraiva as a "lovemark," not just a utility publisher, for lawyers specifically. This ran concurrently with Editora Saraiva's December 2016 visual identity change, also covered by PublishNews — the brand work and the product launch were happening on the same timeline, not sequentially.

Benvirá — the outlier: creative risk in consumer publishing

Benvirá is worth calling out specifically because it's not a higher-ed story — it's evidence of creative range under the same mandate: Brazil's first immersive VR book trailer, built for the launch of a children's book — a format nobody in Brazilian publishing had used before this — and a book trailer for bestselling author Augusto Cury's first teen-oriented novel, betting production value on an already-recognizable name to launch a new format for that audience. Neither needed to happen for the higher-ed mandate to succeed — they show a willingness to take a format risk where the portfolio had the creative latitude for it.
