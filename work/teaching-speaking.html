Teaching, Speaking & Academic Contributions: Turning 25 Years of Practice into Curriculum

Three ways I contribute to the academic field, same underlying discipline: converting hands-on commercial practice into structured, teachable — or assessable — material, not just war stories.

Live University (Ibramerc) — MBA em Inteligência Comercial

Taught the "Gestão de Pipeline" (Pipeline Management) module, including the class "Geração e Qualificação de Leads" (Lead Generation & Qualification) — a full 78-slide curriculum unit, not a single talk. What it covers:

A complete lead taxonomy Spears (active outbound prospecting), Nets (inbound/marketing-driven), Seeds (referral and word-of-mouth) — each with its own advantages and failure modes taught explicitly: Seeds convert at 50%+ but with little control over volume; Nets are highly measurable and scalable but convert at only 1–3%; Spears scale with a trained team but depend on a large, accurate target list.
Persona construction methodology Taught with a full worked example (a fictional persona, "Renata," built out across sociodemographic profile, values/motivations, and needs/objectives) so students build their own rather than just seeing the theory.
The full marketing-to-sales funnel bridge Suspect → Prospect → Marketing Qualified Lead → Sales Accepted Lead → Sales Qualified Lead → Opportunity → Client — diagrammed as a "régua de relacionamento" (relationship ladder), with explicit cadence guidance: roughly a 7-day nurture window for short sales cycles, 1–2 day intervals between touches, scaled proportionally longer for longer cycles.
Lead scoring methodology A–F grading, starting with equal-weighted criteria and iterating based on results, splitting criteria into Profile (role, company size, revenue, existing marketing maturity) and Interest (content downloaded, nurture flows completed, relationship tenure).
BANT qualification Taught with correct attribution to its origin at IBM, and how it promotes an MQL to a true SQL. The deck also includes real friction dialogue between marketing and sales ("they don't follow up on any lead we send" vs. "a lead to them is basically a sale") to make the alignment problem concrete before teaching the fix — built for participation, not lecture-only, with paired group activities throughout.

Guest lecture — Indiana University

Invited by Professor Sunny Zhang to guest lecture in her marketing class: "Global Collaboration: Navigating Cultural Differences for Business Growth." A 12-slide session built on four named academic frameworks, not general cultural-fluency claims:

Four frameworks, used together The Lewis Model (Dimensions of Behaviour), Hall's High-Context/Low-Context model, Hofstede's Cultural Dimensions, and Erin Meyer's Culture Map.
A specific Brazil-vs-USA Hofstede comparison Across all six dimensions — e.g. Power Distance 69 (Brazil) vs. 40 (USA); Individualism 38 vs. 91; Uncertainty Avoidance 76 vs. 46 — turned into three practical contrasts: communication style, time orientation, and negotiation style.
Closed with Erin Meyer's practical guidance Don't underestimate how hard cultural management-style differences are to shift, apply multiple perspectives rather than one default lens, and actively look for what's valuable in an unfamiliar approach instead of just its friction points.

Being invited to teach Erin Meyer's framework at a US university is a different kind of evidence than claiming cross-cultural fluency in a bio.

International Education Evaluator — Cognia

Concurrent with running WAY American School, I served as a named evaluator on real Cognia accreditation site-visit teams — evaluating international schools across Latin America against Cognia's Performance Standards and producing improvement roadmaps. This is a third, distinct way I contribute to the academic field: not teaching a curriculum or presenting a framework, but assessing whether an institution's own practice holds up against an external standard.

A real site-visit team, not a solo credential One documented example: the Our Lady of Mercy School review in Rio de Janeiro (Nov 2020), alongside a full engagement-review team — gathering evidence through stakeholder interviews (governing authority, leaders, teachers, support staff, parents, and students), document review, and classroom observation, then reporting findings back to guide the school's improvement plan.

Note: this evaluator credential reflects my own standing in the field of international education accreditation — it isn't a reflection of WAY American School's own accreditation score, which is a separate result covered in the Centric Learning / WAY American School case study.
