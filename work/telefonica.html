Sr. LATAM Strategic Planning Manager · May 2009 – Jan 2012

First Management Role, Continental Scope: Strategic Planning at Telefónica HQ

A competitive scholarship that put me in Madrid before I ever managed anyone, followed by continental-scope budget ownership and a real multi-country tower-sale negotiation.

Context

This role didn't come out of nowhere. I joined Terra Networks Brasil (part of the Telefónica group) in 2004 as a Product Consultant, and two things happened there that set up everything that followed. First, a six-month job rotation as Marketing Coordinator at Telefónica USA in Miami (2007) — my first time living abroad. Second, and more consequential: I was awarded a full scholarship for the Executive MBA at ESIC Business & Marketing School in Madrid (Aug 2007 – Aug 2008), administered through Fundación Carolina with Telefónica as a sponsoring supporter — one seat, reserved specifically for a promising Telefónica young professional, awarded after competing against thousands of applicants, through TOEFL scoring, language and logical-reasoning assessments, and role-play dynamics. That scholarship is genuine external validation, not a credential I bought — it's what put me in Madrid, fluent in the HQ's expectations, before I ever managed anyone.

That's the run-up to this role: my first management position, and it started with direct exposure to Telefónica's headquarters in Madrid, not a regional office once-removed from decision-making. I was responsible for strategic and financial planning across five Telefónica fixed-line operations in Latin America, reporting straight to HQ. The job was as much translation as strategy: making local market dynamics legible to Madrid, and making HQ strategy actionable for operators on the ground in five different countries.

The challenge

Telefónica's LatAm operations each had their own market conditions, regulatory environment, and commercial pressures, but capital allocation and major asset decisions ran through Madrid. Someone had to own the network operations budget across the whole region under direct HQ supervision — translating between five markets' worth of local reality and one headquarters' worth of financial expectations — while still finding near-term revenue to close budget gaps.

What I did

Owned the LatAm network operations budget under direct HQ supervision This wasn't a country-level assignment — it was continental scope, in my first management role, reporting directly into Madrid. That combination shaped how I built Strategic Planning as a discipline for the rest of my career.
Directed the tower-sale negotiation across Peru and Chile I led the strategic and financial modeling behind selling Telefónica's physical telecom tower infrastructure — 543 towers in Peru and 250 towers in Chile — structured as a sale-and-leaseback, with a 10+5 year rental term. This meant negotiating simultaneously with multiple international infrastructure bidders, each with different pricing logic and risk appetite, while running the NPV analysis that determined whether a given rent-back deal actually made financial sense over a 10–15 year horizon.
Built integrated planning tools Accelerated technology adoption across the regional operations — giving five different markets a shared way to plan technology investment against one another instead of five disconnected roadmaps.
Closed near-term budget gaps Coordinated regional commercial agreements that generated immediate revenue for 2009 rather than only planning for the following year.

What this built

This role is where I built Strategic Planning as an actual discipline, not just a title — continental-scope budget ownership, direct HQ accountability, and a real multi-country deal (bidders, NPV modeling, negotiation) all inside my first two and a half years as a manager. It's also where my international career positioning started: working fluently between Madrid's expectations and five Latin American markets' realities set the pattern for every cross-border role since.

Telefónica reinforced that bet while I was in the role, not just before it: in 2010, midway through this assignment, I was awarded a second full scholarship for a Master's in Telecommunications Business Management — jointly run by Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya and Universitas Telefónica for promising young executives. Two competitively awarded, fully-funded development scholarships in three years is a pattern, not a coincidence.
